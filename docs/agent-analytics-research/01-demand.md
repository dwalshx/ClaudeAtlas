# Research report 1: Demand signals for agent traffic analytics

(Verbatim output of research agent 1, 2026-08-04)

**Research question:** Is there real, expressed demand for tools showing website/API owners how much of their traffic comes from AI agents, crawlers, and bots — and what those machines are doing?

**Method:** 15 web searches/fetches across Hacker News (via hn.algolia.com), vendor announcements, press coverage, Reddit/SEO-community-adjacent content, and product pricing pages. Sources are predominantly 2025–2026.

---

## 1. Direct demand — people explicitly asking "how do I see AI bot traffic?"

**Verdict: real but moderate; the question is asked constantly, but mostly answered by SEO content and free tools rather than by desperate buyers.**

- **"I prompted ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini and watched my Nginx logs"** — [HN, 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47835646). A developer manually reverse-engineered agent behavior from raw logs; commenters engaged with fetch-order patterns ("Claude tends to fetch top-of-message URLs first, while GPT often fetches the last one mentioned"), residential-proxy detection problems for Grok, and one replied "Looks like a very fun exercise, I will try it out as well." This is exactly the target user doing the job by hand — but note it's presented as a fun experiment, not an urgent pain.
- **"Ask HN: How does ChatGPT decide which websites to recommend?"** — [HN, 2026](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46907123) — direct curiosity about agent behavior against one's own site.
- A large "why GA4 can't see this" content industry now exists answering the query: [HoneyLog: "Why AI Bot Traffic Doesn't Show Up in GA4"](https://honeylog.io/blogs/en/ai-bot-traffic-google-analytics) (bots don't execute JS, tag never fires); [AI Crawler Check: "Monitor AI Bot Traffic in Google Analytics & Server Logs"](https://aicrawlercheck.com/blog/monitor-ai-bot-traffic-analytics); [Matomo's own July 2026 "AI Chatbot Traffic Guide"](https://matomo.org/blog/2026/07/ai-chatbot-traffic-guide-for-web-analytics-in-2026/). The volume of SEO content targeting these questions is itself evidence people search them at scale.
- Analytics vendors are responding to expressed user demand: Matomo shipped an AI Assistants detection ecosystem and has a long-standing community [BotTracker plugin](https://github.com/Digitalist-Open-Cloud/Matomo-Plugin-BotTracker) plus a ["Re-allow tracking bots" issue (#17497)](https://github.com/matomo-org/matomo/issues/17497); Adobe shipped an ["Agentic Traffic" dashboard](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/llm-optimizer/using/dashboards/agentic-traffic) requiring CDN log forwarding; there are Adobe Analytics community threads asking about ["Impact of AI agents & ChatGPT"](https://experienceleaguecommunities-beta.adobe.com/adobe-analytics-3/impact-of-ai-agents-chatgpt-93152).
- **Cautionary data point:** small indie launches in exactly this niche got near-zero HN traction: "Ask HN: Beta testers for a free AI crawler analytics dashboard" (5 points, Nov 2025), "Show HN: 1,011 AI crawler requests. Google Analytics saw zero" (1 point, Mar 2026, [adwait.me](https://www.adwait.me/writings/tracking-ai-crawlers-server-side)), "Show HN: self-hosted web analytics with AI crawler tracking" (1 point, Jul 2026, [github.com/DigiHold/insight](https://github.com/DigiHold/insight)), "Show HN: We're tracking AI bot visits daily" (2 points, Oct 2025, [llmscentral.com](https://llmscentral.com)). Multiple people are *building* this; almost nobody is upvoting it.

## 2. Pain narratives — viral AI-crawler suffering

**Verdict: very strong and sustained, but the pain expressed is overwhelmingly about *cost and abuse*, not about *measurement*. The demanded remedy is blocking, not analytics.**

- **Wikimedia (Apr 2025):** 50% bandwidth increase since Jan 2024 from AI scrapers; 2025/26 annual plan targets reducing crawler traffic 20% ([Wikimedia Diff, 2025-04-01](https://diff.wikimedia.org/2025/04/01/how-crawlers-impact-the-operations-of-the-wikimedia-projects/); [TechCrunch, 2025-04-02](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/02/ai-crawlers-cause-wikimedia-commons-bandwidth-demands-to-surge-50); [The Register, 2025-04-03](https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/03/wikimedia_foundation_bemoans_bot_bandwidth/)).
- **SourceHut (Mar 2025):** Drew DeVault described bots crawling "every page of every git log... using random User-Agents that actively and maliciously adapt and blend in with end-user traffic" ([The Register, 2025-03-18](https://www.theregister.com/2025/03/18/ai_crawlers_sourcehut/)). ReadTheDocs, iFixit, and Diaspora made similar complaints ([Techdirt, 2025-04-10](https://www.techdirt.com/2025/04/10/ai-crawlers-are-harming-wikimedia-bringing-open-source-sites-to-their-knees-and-putting-the-open-web-at-risk/)).
- **iFixit vs ClaudeBot (Jul 2024):** ~1M hits in 24 hours; CEO Kyle Wiens publicly complained about DevOps resources being tied up ([Newsbytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/anthropic-s-ai-scraped-ifixit-s-site-million-times-breaching-terms/story); [The Register, 2024-07-30](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/07/30/taming-ai-content-crawlers-is-no-longer-a-manual-affair/1203397)).
- **"AI companies cause most of traffic on forums"** — [HN, Dec 2024, 770 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42549624). Operator of ~30 forums: "4.8M hits from Claude, 390k from Amazon... 148k from ChatGPT" in 7 days; "2M requests arrived on December 23rd from Claude alone for a single site." Another commenter: "My website was absolutely destroyed by Meta's AI bot... until my server crashed." Notably, the operator *could already measure this from logs* — the ask was for relief, not dashboards.
- **Anubis** (proof-of-work firewall) went viral precisely because of this pain: one Forgejo server went from ~600K bot requests/day to ~100 after deploying it; now runs in front of GNOME, KDE, FFmpeg, Codeberg ([Lobsters](https://lobste.rs/s/sknzdg/anubis_self_hostable_scraper_defense); [Codeberg/Forgejo discussion #319](https://codeberg.org/forgejo/discussions/issues/319)). Again: the winning viral product in the pain space is a *blocker*, not an analytics tool.
- **Cloudflare vs Perplexity (Aug 2025):** Cloudflare accused Perplexity of stealth-crawling with disguised user agents and rotated ASNs after blocks ([HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44793772); [Medium summary](https://medium.com/@venkatagopikolla/the-cloudflare-perplexity-standoff-why-robots-txt-is-broken-for-the-ai-era-1b9d309bdc2b)). Cloudflare also documented a 1,700:1 crawl-to-referral ratio for one AI company (Jun 2025) — that *ratio stat* is an analytics artifact that went viral, suggesting attribution numbers do resonate when someone else computes them.

## 3. Adjacent demand — the AEO/GEO wave

**Verdict: this is where the money and pull overwhelmingly are. "Is ChatGPT citing/sending me customers?" has an order of magnitude more commercial demand than "what are bots doing on my server?"**

- **Profound** raised **$155M+ total with a $1B valuation Series C** (early 2026) for AI-visibility tracking, and notably added an ["Agent Analytics" crawler-traffic feature](https://www.tryprofound.com/features/agent-analytics) as a *feature inside* an AI-visibility platform ([Startup Intros](https://startupintros.com/orgs/profound)). Pricing: $99–$399/mo self-serve, ~$2,000+/mo for real multi-platform coverage ([Workduo pricing review, 2026](https://www.workduo.ai/blog/profound-ai-pricing)).
- **Peec AI**: €1.8M pre-seed (Apr 2025) → €5.2M seed (5 weeks later) → $21M Series A (Nov 2025); reportedly adding ~300 customers/month ([MaxAEO review](https://maxaeo.ai/blog/peec-ai-review-2026-best-for-ai-visibility-monitoring-use-cases-limits-alternatives/)). A dense comparison-content ecosystem (Profound vs Peec vs Otterly, etc.) signals heavy buyer search volume.
- **GA4 AI-referral tracking** is a huge how-to genre: `utm_source=chatgpt.com` setup guides, custom "AI Traffic" channel groups, complaints that ~33% of AI-search sessions arrive with no referrer and get bucketed as Direct/(not set) ([Clickport, 2026](https://clickport.io/blog/chatgpt-direct-traffic-ga4); [Conversios 2026 guide](https://www.conversios.io/blog/track-ai-referral-traffic-from-chatgpt-in-ga4/); [SurferSEO](https://surferseo.com/blog/track-chatgpt-ai-traffic-in-google-analytics/)).
- The crawler-analytics and visibility framings are converging: **Dark Visitors rebranded to "Known Agents" in Feb 2026**, explicitly repositioning "from defensive 'block the bots' to... 'understand and optimize for AI agents as a growth channel'" ([knownagents.com](https://knownagents.com); [WordPress plugin](https://wordpress.org/plugins/dark-visitors/)). That a pioneer of pure agent-traffic analytics pivoted its framing toward AI-growth/AEO is a strong market signal about which story sells. Its HN reception mirrors this: the original 2023 launch got 134 points; the 2025 free-plan and 2026 rebrand posts got 1–2 points ([Algolia data](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22Dark%20Visitors%22)).

## 4. Willingness to pay / build

**Verdict: demonstrated — but concentrated in (a) marketing budgets for visibility and (b) publisher monetization, not in a standalone "server-side analytics" SKU.**

- **AEO tools**: Profound $99–$2,000+/mo with $155M raised; Peec growing 300 customers/mo (above). One GEO agency raised pricing to **$10,000/month** and capped client capacity ([press release, May 2025](https://markets.financialcontent.com/publicradio/article/kisspr-2025-5-22-generative-search-visibility-caps-client-capacity-raises-pricing-to-10000-per-month)).
- **TollBit** (bot paywalls/pay-per-crawl): 730% growth in bot-paywall adoption Q4'24→Q1'25, 3,000+ websites, publishers earning "up to tens of thousands of dollars per month"; Washington Post's Arc XP integrated it (Apr 2026); Akamai integrated TollBit + Skyfire at the network layer (Sep 2025) ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/the-washington-posts-arc-xp-adds-tollbit-to-help-publishers-make-money-from-ai-bot-traffic/); [Akamai press release](https://www.akamai.com/newsroom/press-release/no-free-crawls-akamai-tollbit-and-skyfire-turn-traffic-into-revenue)). Note that monetization *requires* the measurement layer — TollBit markets "AI bot traffic tracking" as the on-ramp ([mediacopilot.ai](https://mediacopilot.ai/why-publishers-choose-tollbit-to-monitor-ai-bot-traffic/)).
- **Vercel** monetizes adjacent bot detection: BotID Deep Analysis (Kasada) at **$1/1,000 calls** on Pro/Enterprise ([Vercel blog](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-botid)).
- **DIY behavior**: developers hand-rolling nginx-log analysis ([adwait.me](https://www.adwait.me/writings/tracking-ai-crawlers-server-side), the HN nginx-logs post, [ranthebuilder.cloud "My Blog's Biggest Fans Are AI Bots"](https://ranthebuilder.cloud/blog/my-blogs-biggest-fans-are-ai-bots-so-i-fought-back/)) — evidence of the job-to-be-done, though DIY-with-grep is also a substitute.

## 5. Counter-signals — free tools eating the category

**Verdict: substantial. The pure "see your AI bot traffic" layer is being commoditized fast by platforms that already sit on the logs.**

- **Cloudflare AI Crawl Control** (formerly AI Audit) is GA, free-tier-inclusive, shows per-crawler activity, splits traffic into Search/Agent/Training, and bundles blocking + pay-per-crawl ([Cloudflare blog, 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/introducing-ai-crawl-control/); [Help Net Security, Jul 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/02/cloudflare-ai-crawler-controls/)). From Sep 15, 2026 it *blocks* Training/Agent bots by default on ad-supported pages — Cloudflare is shaping defaults, not just observing.
- **Vercel Observability** added per-bot and bot-category breakdowns including AI crawlers "across all plans" ([changelog](https://vercel.com/changelog/bot-activity-and-crawler-insights-now-in-observability)), plus a published taxonomy of ["the three types of AI bot traffic"](https://vercel.com/blog/the-three-types-of-ai-bot-traffic-and-how-to-handle-them).
- **Ahrefs Bot Analytics** launched Feb–Mar 2026: every bot, every page, 12 bot categories, an "AI bots" filter — **free during beta, no paid plan needed** (requires Cloudflare Logpush/Worker integration) ([Ahrefs blog](https://ahrefs.com/blog/new-features-february-march-2026/); [product page](https://ahrefs.com/bot-analytics)).
- Incumbent CMS/analytics vendors shipping it as a feature: Adobe LLM Optimizer Agentic Traffic dashboard, Matomo AI Assistants detection, Zuora Zephr "AI Bot Traffic Monitor" ([docs](https://docs.zuora.com/en/zephr/analyze-insights/analyze-insights/ai-bot-traffic-monitor)).
- HN engagement asymmetry (Section 1): pain posts about crawler abuse hit 770 points; analytics-tool Show HNs hit 1–5 points. People upvote the problem, not the dashboard.
- Structural headwind for accuracy: the worst offenders deliberately evade UA-based measurement (SourceHut's "random User-Agents that... blend in," Perplexity's disguised Chrome UA) — so a drop-in UA-parsing product measures the polite bots and misses the abusive ones, which is the part people are angriest about.

---

## Honest verdict

Demand for *something* in this space is unambiguous — AI-machine traffic is now a majority of many sites' requests, the pain narratives are viral and sustained (Wikimedia, SourceHut, iFixit, Anubis), and platforms from Cloudflare to Ahrefs to Adobe are all shipping bot-visibility features in 2025–2026, which is the strongest possible confirmation that customers ask for it. But the *expressed, paying* demand splits sharply by framing: pure "agent traffic analytics" (see what machines hit your server) is a real but weakly-monetizing utility that is being commoditized to free by whoever already owns the logs (Cloudflare free tier, Vercel all-plans, Ahrefs free beta), and indie versions of exactly this product repeatedly land with 1–5 points on HN; whereas the adjacent framings command real money — **AI visibility/citation tracking** (Profound $155M raised at $1B, Peec $29M in 12 months, $99–$10k/mo price points) and **bot monetization/control** (TollBit 730% growth, Akamai integration, publishers earning five figures monthly). Dark Visitors — the closest existing product to the proposed idea — pivoting its brand from bot-watching to "AI agents as a growth channel" is the market telling you the answer. Recommendation: the drop-in "see your invisible machine traffic" capability is a compelling *wedge and demo* ("GA saw zero of these 1,011 requests" is a great hook), but the durable expressed demand attaches to what the data enables — AI-search attribution, citation/visibility insight, or crawl monetization/blocking — not to the traffic dashboard itself, and any standalone analytics play must survive Cloudflare and Ahrefs giving 80% of it away for free.
